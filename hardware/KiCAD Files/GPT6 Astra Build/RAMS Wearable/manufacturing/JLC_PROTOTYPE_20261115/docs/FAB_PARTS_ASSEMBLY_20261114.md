## QFN exposed-pad and stencil decision

**Final integration, November 15:** the top-mask/paste change is implemented and audited. Use sixteen chamfered windows and 61.42% paste coverage; the initial square-window figures below describe the inspected input. See FAB_MASK_REVIEW_20261114.md and the final independent mask report for the accepted geometry.

The inspected board has a 5.7×5.7 mm continuous exposed ground land; nine 0.20 mm drilled, 0.45 mm diameter vias at local X/Y=-1.4,0,+1.4 mm; and sixteen 1.12 mm paste windows at X/Y=-2.1,-0.7,+0.7,+2.1 mm. Thus the existing paste coverage is `16×1.12²/5.7²=61.8%`. A 0.10 mm stencil gives the 0.75×0.20 mm perimeter aperture an area ratio of `0.75×0.20/[2×0.10×(0.75+0.20)]=0.789`.

**Keep the nine ground vias.** [Espressif's ESP32-S3 PCB guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32s3/pcb-layout-design.html), Power Supply, recommends at least nine; simply removing them to avoid solder loss abandons its grounding recommendation. Ordinary via-tenting flags do not override a full exposed-pad mask opening.

For the low-cost process, root owns the actual CAD correction: implement a real top-mask crosshatch around the via holes and verify the exported mask/paste geometry. [TI SLUA271C, §3.4.1, page 7](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slua271c/slua271c.pdf) supports top-side mask tenting/crosshatch, specifies a mask diameter at least 0.10 mm larger than the hole, and warns that backside-only coverage can increase trapped-air voiding. For the current 0.20 mm hole, the nominal covered disc must therefore be at least 0.30 mm diameter, with fabrication registration allowance considered. Keep the useful continuous copper underneath; mask segmentation is not copper segmentation. Final mask/paste dimensions and test results must be recorded by the integrating editor, rather than treating this recommendation as an implemented change.

[JLC's via-covering specification](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-via-covering) excludes via-in-pad and vias within 0.35 mm of pads from ordinary ink plugging. Filled-and-capped epoxy is a valid alternative, but [JLC states it is charged for four-layer boards](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/via-filling-guide). It is not automatically needed when a manufacturable top-mask design is chosen. Do not call back tenting, ink plugging and epoxy filling equivalent. A mask-based first article still needs joint/process validation after assembly; it is a selected low-cost method, not guaranteed elimination of voids.

## Heatbed evidence and practical assembly gate

The Gemini-era instructions are in repository path `hardware/KiCAD Files/Antigravity Build/RAMS - PCB/JLCPCB_ORDER_PACKAGE/PARTS_LIST_FOR_ORDERING.md`, under “DIY Reflow Option”: **Mechanic IX5 Ultra**, **Relife RL-403 Sn63Pb37**, 140–150°C for 60–90 s, then 205–210°C for 30–45 s. Their former FS8205A eight-pin, SS14W and buzzer descriptions are superseded by this board's checked BOM. The paste/heatbed names are historical instructions, not proof of what is currently on the bench. The current parts inventory contains no paste alloy, powder-size, expiry, storage record or thermocouple profile.

Use the 0.10 mm top stencil and a paste documented for fine apertures. Before populating an expensive complete board, check the actual syringe label and perform one print/reflow trial on a spare board, observing clean release at the 0.20 mm QFN apertures, no inter-pad bridges and component-temperature behavior. Heatbed display temperature is not the package's temperature. Follow the actual paste's published profile; the old 205–210°C recipe is only a starting reference if the paste is indeed Sn63Pb37. No primary RL-403 profile was established in this review. A different alloy requires its own profile, not a guessed temperature increment. [TI's QFN assembly guidance](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slua271c/slua271c.pdf), §§2.1 and 5.2, makes the profile dependent on the actual paste, board and component limits.

Reflow top electronics with bottom Ra-02 absent. Keep BZ1 out of reflow as already specified; add it by iron afterward. Leave LED1 and R30 unpopulated for first power. Cool and inspect before turning the board over into a holder to solder the Ra-02 castellations. Never put the completed bottom module directly against the heatbed. Do not scrape or force a thermal-pad fillet beneath U1 to “prove” the hidden joint; visible perimeter wetting cannot certify the exposed-pad attachment.
