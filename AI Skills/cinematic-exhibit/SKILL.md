# Cinematic exhibit

Design the opening frame around the question an audience member should be able to answer. In the RAMS exhibit the question is “How does an alert get off this road?” A thin amber path through cyan terrain makes that relationship visible before the speaker says a word. Choose the equivalent physical feature, record or transformation for the new subject. Use the [opening composition](references/corridor.jpg) and [inspection composition](references/inspection.jpg) as visual references.

## Establish the frame

- In a wide exhibit, keep the thesis and advance control in one reading column. Let the subject occupy the larger part of the stage. Set the camera where two or more meaningful surfaces are legible. Protect a quiet region behind text. On a phone, stack the subject and its controls in reading order. Check the actual projector size before settling on heading size and stage height.
- For a close match, start with the original canvas `#080D12`, raised surfaces `#0E171E`, rules `#29333C`, text `#F1F5F7`, secondary text `#A1AEB9`, selected controls `#75E5E3` and event highlight `#FFC27A`. Preserve the target product’s semantics for warning, selection and error. Use a geometric sans for narration and tabular or monospaced figures for time, units and data.
- In a native app or a dense dashboard, give the strongest spatial emphasis to the selected object, chart, record or next action. Preserve the platform’s navigation, focus order, accessible names, scaling and existing state. Keep empty and disconnected states recognizable. Import only the visual decisions that help the task.
- For a research or hardware exhibit, match the revision, dimensions, data origin and screen state to primary evidence. Caption a reconstruction, fictional operator record or synthetic trace where it appears. Reserve installation and performance claims for evidence from the corresponding system.

## Choose where the scene runs

A surface that visitors can orbit or a packet whose destination they can select benefits from live geometry. A fixed choreography with detailed people, moving vehicles or costly reflections can be rendered ahead of time, then played locally through a native video element with the same timeline and phase captions. Keep overview navigation, data controls and speaker notes in the accessible UI layer in either case.

For a landscape, hardware inspection or glass material, read [Scene construction](references/scene-construction.md). It covers branching vegetation, shot lighting, scans, water, glass and frame pacing. For the user's original references and their specific uses, read [Visual studies](references/visual-studies.md).

## Make interaction demonstrate a result

### Carry recognizable objects across chapters

When a diagram revisits hardware the audience has already inspected, load the same board revision and geometry. Preserve the connector positions, board outline and major components. Reuse the same asset for an offline render if the close-up is too expensive to draw live. For a phone or station monitor, put a capture of the relevant product screen inside the display area. Keep recorded values and fixture labels intact. A generic chassis is sufficient when the enclosure itself is illustrative.

Set the three objects on the same ground plane as the route. Carry its lighting direction, line weight, point density and selection color into supports and edges; reserve bright screen content for the active shot. Frame the identifying feature at the actual venue resolution. Have someone move from the inspection chapter to the handoff and identify the board, the source of the phone reading and the destination of the packet from the pictures alone.

For an incident film, establish where the person starts and lands. Separate what the camera shows from what the sensor can infer. If replacing the actor, check both the artist's mesh license and the character owner's terms. A textured character in a T-pose may need a skeleton. Inspect a close-up of the hand holding the prop, a wide shot of the moving figure and the final ground contact before rendering the sequence. Long hair, sleeves and equipment need to follow the joints that carry them. Take offline fallback stills from the same finished frames.

Give each control an observable outcome. A board flip reveals the radio on its underside. A relay step moves the camera from sender to receiver while the label follows the selected device. Starting another move should interrupt the old shot from the current pose. Revisit a chapter and reset its controls deliberately. For reduced motion, snap to the selected view and keep its caption and primary action.

If a presenter will operate it, keep one ordered set of cues shared by the website and rehearsal documents. Pair the exact control label and gesture with a sentence the presenter can say as the result becomes visible. Include pause, replay, scrub, flip, reset, alternate selections and handoffs that exist in the exhibit. Read the timed version aloud while clicking, and move optional exploration into the question period.

## Verify the result in use

Open the deliverable through the audience’s normal entry point. Inspect wide and narrow layouts with the actual text. Test the complete selection → inspection → return workflow, interrupt an animation halfway, use the keyboard or native equivalent, and switch on reduced motion. For an offline build, extract the packaged copy into a new folder, disable networking and exercise the fallback views. Record the device or renderer, viewport and observed behavior wherever a performance claim matters.
